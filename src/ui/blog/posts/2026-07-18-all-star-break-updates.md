A few things shipped over the All-Star Break. Nothing flashy for its own sake, mostly the kind of midseason quality-of-life that makes team and player pages easier to read when you're catching up between series.

## Series Calendar

Team pages now have a **Series Calendar**: every series of the season in one list, with home/away, opponent logos, and game-by-game W/L color. It's the schedule viewed the way baseball people talk about it: not day-by-day, but series-by-series.

![Series Calendar on the Dodgers team page](/screenshots/series-calendar.png)

Try it on any [team page](/teams), e.g. the [Dodgers](/teams/119).

## Season Splits

Player pages now show a **season splits** table above the year-by-year charts: per-season hitting or pitching lines, career totals, team logos, and a little positive/negative coloring so the outliers jump out.

If a player changed teams midseason, each stint gets its own row, with the most recent team on top.

![Season splits table on Shohei Ohtani's player page](/screenshots/season-splits.png)

See it on any [player page](/player). [Ohtani](/player/660271) is a good example.

## Year-by-year hover guides

Hovering a year-by-year chart now draws dashed vertical and horizontal guides through the active point, with a neutral value label so you can read a season without guessing against the min/max callouts.

![Year-by-year chart with hover guides](/screenshots/year-by-year-hover.png)

## Hot/cold zones

Hot/cold strike zones use a diverging scale now: **green for hot, red for cold**, transparent in the middle. Cold zones used to fade into the same green ramp, which made them harder to read at a glance.

![Hot/cold zones with red-to-green scale](/screenshots/hot-cold-zones.png)
